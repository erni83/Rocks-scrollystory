## 1. The Dry Corridor
*Shupá, Guatemala · February 28, 2026*

Gregorio Pérez García is sixty — a farmer, community leader, and local reporter who has worked this ground most of his life. The corridor running from southern Mexico through Guatemala, Honduras, and El Salvador is one of the most climate-punished stretches of land in the Western Hemisphere. Here, everything depends on water.

## 3. The Cost of the Harvest — "La Creatina"
*Parcelamiento El Cajón, sector Agüero, Guatemala · March 2, 2026*

Along the Pacific lowlands of Central America, young men have been dying of kidney failure for decades — healthy men in their twenties and thirties, with none of the usual precursors. Researchers call it CKDnT: chronic kidney disease of non-traditional origin. Communities call it *La Creatina*, after the test that measures kidney function.

**`HEA26002_02596`**
- **Short:** Edvin Rafael Sarmiento, 32, at his family's home — sector Agüero, Guatemala.
- **Extended:** Edvin cut sugarcane until his kidneys failed six years ago; he has not worked since. He lives beside the fields that made him sick, among the family who now help support him.

**`HEA26002_02985`**
- **Short:** Edvin in the doorway of his home, his family gathered behind him — sector Agüero, Guatemala.
- **Extended:** CKDnT strikes young, previously healthy men doing heavy labor in extreme heat. There is no cure; the only treatments — dialysis and transplant — are rarely available where cane is cut. Globally, the toll exceeds 26 million people.

**`HEA26002_02691`**  *(sensitive)*
- **Short:** A woman sits with an untreated leg injury as a young mother and child stay close by — sector Agüero, Guatemala.
- **Extended:** An old injury became an infection and, without ready access to health care, was left to worsen. It is a scene that repeats across these communities, where poverty and distance from care turn a treatable problem into a lasting one.

**`HEA26002_03486`**
- **Short:** A worker opens an irrigation line in a young cane field — sector El Betania, Guatemala.
- **Extended:** Irrigation crews spend the day moving water across the fields under open sky. The work is relentless and the heat constant — conditions that, over a season, quietly damage the kidneys of the people doing it.

**`HEA26002_02298`**
- **Short:** A cane worker leaves a roadside shop with a three-liter cola — sector Agüero, Guatemala.
- **Extended:** In the middle of a punishing shift, the quickest thing to buy is sugar, not hydration. La Isla Network's research has documented how sugary drinks — and, in some fields, painkillers sold to workers — can worsen the very kidney damage the heat is already causing.

## 8. Rest, Shade, Hydration
*Guazapa, El Salvador · March 7, 2026*

The intervention is simple, and its simplicity is the point: scheduled rest in shade, water on a schedule, and real-time monitoring of heat strain.

**`HEA26002_07930`**
- **Short:** Cutters rest under a shade tent with a water cooler, fields and mountains beyond — Guazapa, El Salvador.
- **Extended:** Tarps, hoses, coolers, shade cloth — the infrastructure of protection is unglamorous, it costs very little, and it changes everything.

**`HEA26002_07986`**
- **Short:** Workers in La Isla Network's PREP program take a scheduled break — Guazapa, El Salvador.
- **Extended:** Workers drink on a schedule, because by the time a person feels thirsty in extreme heat, kidney stress has already begun. In Nicaragua, these protocols cut acute kidney injury by more than 94 percent.

**`HEA26002_08051`**
- **Short:** A crew gathers in the shade through the hottest part of the day — Guazapa, El Salvador.
- **Extended:** Productivity actually rose 9 to 19 percent under the protocols, even with the added breaks, and one mill reached a 60 percent return on investment by year three. Protecting workers turned out to be the better business decision.

**`HEA26002_06882`**
- **Short:** Workers refill a water cooler from a tank truck beside the buses — Guazapa, El Salvador.
- **Extended:** Clean water, resupplied through the day, is the backbone of the program. The logistics are unglamorous and decisive.

**`HEA26002_06935`**
- **Short:** Filling the day's water supply at the edge of the field — Guazapa, El Salvador.
- **Extended:** With The Rockefeller Foundation, La Isla Network is extending this model to 1,300 sugarcane workers and their 5,850 family members in El Salvador.

**`HEA26002_07541`**
- **Short:** A worker sleeps in a hammock strung inside a parked bus, out of the sun — Guazapa, El Salvador.
- **Extended:** Rest is part of the protocol, not a luxury. Getting out of the heat during peak hours is one of the cheapest, most effective protections there is.

**`HEA26002_08612`**
- **Short:** A field-team member keeps watch over the crew from the shade — Guazapa, El Salvador.
- **Extended:** Behind the protocols is a team measuring what used to be invisible — heat, exertion, and physiological strain, worker by worker. That data is what turns a worker's testimony into evidence institutions cannot dismiss.
